# Brief — Why Octopuses Are So Smart

_Created 2026-10-27 · project `videos/octopus-intelligence/`_

## The video in one sentence
A fast, wonder-filled explainer on how an animal with no skeleton, a 1–2 year life, and no parents to teach it evolved one of the most alien intelligences on Earth — and what that tells us about minds in general.

## Audience
- Who: curious general audience, 16–45, who watch science explainers (Kurzgesagt / Veritasium / Real Science viewers). (proposed)
- What they already know: octopuses have eight arms, are "smart", can change color. Probably seen one escape-artist clip.
- What they want from this video: the *why* — a few jaw-dropping facts plus one big idea they can repeat to a friend.

- Type: explainer

- Aspect ratio: 16:9 (1920x1080)
- Tone & style: playful wonder, conversational, a little cheeky; accurate, no hype beyond the evidence. (proposed)

## Key points (must cover)
1. **Distributed brain** — ~500 million neurons, about two-thirds of them in the arms; each arm can taste, touch and act semi-independently.
2. **Alien origin** — our last common ancestor was a tiny worm-like creature ~600 million years ago; octopus intelligence evolved completely separately from ours (convergent evolution).
3. **Problem-solving & tool use** — opening jars, escaping tanks (Inky, New Zealand, 2016), veined octopuses carrying coconut shells as portable shelters.
4. **Master of disguise** — color and texture change in a fraction of a second, despite likely being colorblind.
5. **Why smart? The big idea** — losing the shell: a soft, defenseless body in a hostile reef forced flexible problem-solving and camouflage over armor.
6. **The paradox** — they live only 1–2 years and never meet their parents; every octopus figures the world out alone.

## Hook idea
Cold open: "This animal has nine brains, three hearts, blue blood… and it can taste with its arms." → "But the weirdest thing isn't its body. It's that it's smart at all."
